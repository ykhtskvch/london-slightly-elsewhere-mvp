Terms used here

Words this site uses on purpose.

Not a style guide. Each of these is doing a specific job, and most of them are there to protect you from a bad day out.

Old room
Not the same as an old pub. An old pub can be ruined by one refit; an old room survives its owners. You know one when your voice drops slightly on entering.
Easy exit
A named station within a short walk of every stage, and — more importantly — permission. Leaving a route early is a version of the route, not a failure of it.
The useful version
What the route page owes you: names, minutes, and what to do when the pub is full. Mood is the reason to go; the useful version is how.
What not to expect
Written before what to expect, and taken more seriously. Most bad days out are caused by accurate places and inaccurate hopes.
Field-checked
Walked, in person, recently enough to say so with a date. Anything else on this site is labelled a prototype, because assembling a route from knowledge is not the same as standing in it.
Prototype
An honest draft. The shape is right; the details have not been stood next to. Treat every fact on a prototype as a rumour with good posture.
A warm finish
The last stop, chosen so that the day has an ending rather than a trailing-off. Usually an old room. Never a queue.
Slightly elsewhere
The feeling this site is for. Not another city — just enough sideways distance from your usual London that the evening acquires a border, and therefore a shape.
