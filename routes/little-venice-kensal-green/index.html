Little Venice to Harlesden: Angels, Parrots and Galicia

A westward walk through canals, cemetery mysteries, eccentric interiors, parrots and one unpredictable Galician restaurant.

Good to know.

  • Check Kensal Green Cemetery’s current gate time and remember that it is an active cemetery, not only a heritage site.
  • The canal and Roundwood Park are exposed; wet weather makes the long outdoor middle less appealing.
  • Confirm Centro Galego is open on the day rather than building dinner around remembered hours.
  • Check The Lexi’s current programme if a film is part of the plan.
  • Brent lists public toilets in Roundwood Park, but works or temporary access changes may affect availability; use venue toilets as backup.

We never ask for your location.

West London gradually loosens its tie: canal edges, cemetery angels, community institutions, municipal parrots and a Galician room near the railway.

Start among the narrowboats of Little Venice and keep walking west: the pretty canal becomes a more functional one, Victorian monuments give way to a darkly theatrical room, and community Kensal Rise leads to a municipal hill with parrots. If the opening hours agree with reality, the walk lands over Galician food in Harlesden before the short practical finish at Willesden Junction.

Who it suits, and who it does not.

  • A long afternoon that can become dinner
  • A date or small group who like noticing odd details
  • Someone who knows central west London but not what lies beyond it

Not ideal for

  • A late start after the cemetery and park have lost their daylight
  • A step-free promise without checking live access
  • Anyone who needs every venue to keep predictable hours

Not a conventional tourist itinerary and not a pub crawl. Paradise is one atmospheric room; Centro Galego is the food finale.

On a date, with friends, or alone

On a date

Good for a second or third date that wants a real walk, several changes of atmosphere and one meal rather than a sequence of drinks.

With friends

Best for two to four people who are happy to keep moving west and decide before Kensal Rise whether the film branch is actually happening.

Alone

Strong alone in daylight: the canal and cemetery give it shape, the library and park keep it local, and Willesden Junction is a practical finish.

The route.

  1. Warwick Avenue → Little Venice

    start · 10–15 min · 2–5 min to next

    Warwick Avenue station and Little Venice basin, London W2

    From the start Leave Warwick Avenue station and walk south to the canal basin, then turn west rather than circling the water.

    Give the basin ten minutes: narrowboats, bridges, still water and a very polished version of west London. Its job is to set the baseline. The route becomes less curated from here.

  2. Grand Union Canal – the long transition west

    walk · 40–50 min · 5–10 min to next

  3. Kensal Green Cemetery – monuments and two crematorium mysteries

    walk · 45–60 min · 15–20 min to next

    Kensal Green Cemetery, Harrow Road, London W10

    From the previous stop Leave the canal for the public cemetery entrance and check the gate time before committing to the interior.

    Opened in 1833 and influenced by Père-Lachaise, the oldest of London’s Magnificent Seven is a city of Victorian monuments, mausoleums and listed structures. Isambard Kingdom Brunel, Charles Babbage and Harold Pinter are enough of a roll call. Alan Rickman was cremated at West London Crematorium within the complex; his ashes went back to his family. Freddie Mercury was cremated here too, although what happened to his ashes afterwards remains one of London’s more persistent cemetery mysteries. A small plaque naming Farrokh Bulsara appeared here and later disappeared: treat that as an unresolved story, not a burial location.

  4. Paradise by way of Kensal Green – find the Reading Room

    pub · 30–45 min · 15–20 min to next

    19 Kilburn Lane, London W10 4AE

    From the previous stop Exit towards Harrow Road and use the live crossings to reach 19 Kilburn Lane.

    Come for the rooms, not to turn the route into a crawl. Portraits, chandeliers, dark furniture and church-like references make it theatrical, slightly sinister and decadent rather than cosy. Find the Reading Room: the library looks more scholarly than it really is, but the room is still worth finding. The venue currently presents itself online as The Belvedere@Paradise, so check the current name, access and programme before going.

  5. Kensal Rise Library – the one the neighbourhood brought back

    view · 10–15 min · 20–25 min to next

    Bathurst Gardens, London NW10 5JA

    From the previous stop Continue north through Kensal Rise to Bathurst Gardens at College Road.

    A neighbourhood library opened by Mark Twain in 1900, closed more than a century later, then effectively fought back into existence by the people around it. It now runs independently with volunteer support. Check the live hours if you want to go inside; the community story is the stop.

    Want to turn the walk into a film night?

    The Lexi Cinema

    A volunteer-supported independent cinema and social enterprise rooted in Kensal Rise; its distributable profits go to charity. Use it as a bad-weather branch, an alternative ending or a deliberate film night, not a compulsory two-hour pause before dinner. Check the live programme rather than relying on a remembered title.

  6. Roundwood Park – The Knowle and the aviary

    walk · 35–50 min · 15–20 min to next

    Roundwood Park, Harlesden Road, London NW10 3SH

    From the previous stop Walk north-west through the residential streets to the Harlesden Road side of the park; take the Lexi branch first only if using it.

    This late-Victorian municipal park opened in 1895 and earns its place three times. Climb The Knowle: it is not much of a mountain, but London is flat enough that the view towards Wembley is surprisingly decent. Then find the modest aviary, where small parrots and other birds provide one of the walk’s odder discoveries. Brent lists public toilets here, although access may still vary while renovation work finishes.

    Optional daytime stop

    Roundwood Lodge Cafe

    Simple, old-school and useful for coffee or a panini rather than speciality brunch. It often closes relatively early, so treat the cafe as a bonus rather than a plan.

  7. Centro Galego de Londres – Galicia for dinner

    dinner · 60–90 min · 7–10 min to next

    869 Harrow Road, London NW10 5NG

    From the previous stop Leave Roundwood Park towards Harrow Road and follow the live route to number 869.

    This is not simply a Spanish restaurant: it is tied to London’s Galician community and feels like a social and cultural centre with food. The room is familial, communal and unpolished in the useful sense; dinner is the reason to finish here. People who love it tend to be unusually enthusiastic about it. Check that they are actually open before setting off. Opening hours here should be treated more as useful information than a binding contract.

Before you go.

Best time

Afternoon into evening, preferably dry

Food and drink

Paradise is an atmospheric pause, Roundwood Lodge is an early-closing bonus, and Centro Galego is the meal. Do not try to eat at all three.

How the day can change.

The full westward walk

Canal, cemetery, Paradise, community Kensal Rise, Roundwood Park and the checked Centro Galego finish.

5–7 hours · £30–£70

Walk without dinner

Keep the full westward line but treat Centro Galego as a waypoint and finish at Willesden Junction.

4–5 hours · under £30

Lexi film ending

Leave the main route in Kensal Rise for a booked screening; this is an alternative ending, not a prelude to rushing the park and dinner.

4½–7 hours · £30–£70 plus ticket · ticket check required

If it rains

Shorten the canal and park, and use Paradise or The Lexi for shelter; if the long outdoor middle disappears, admit that this is a different day rather than claiming the full route survived.

If it goes well

Keep Centro Galego and the straightforward Willesden Junction ending. The route has already found its final room; it does not need another bar.

If you want to leave early

Use Kensal Green after the cemetery or Paradise, or Kensal Rise around the library and Lexi branch.
