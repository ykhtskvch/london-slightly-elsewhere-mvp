The rare kind of route where the Tube takes you out, then the water does the quiet work of the day.

Ride the furthest-out end of the Metropolitan line and start with the small, ordinary edge of Chesham. Then the River Chess begins to pull the day into shape: green water, meadows, manor country and quiet village lanes. The pub in Chenies is the one grown-up pause. From there, return to the water, keep walking east and let Rickmansworth arrive as the cleanest possible finish.

Best for.

  • A pair who want a proper walking day without National Rail logistics
  • A solo reset with one clear navigation line
  • Anyone who likes villages more than destination restaurants

Not ideal for.

  • Wet weeks or flood warnings
  • A first outing in brand-new shoes
  • Anyone who needs cafés and public toilets every hour

Read this before the platform.

Terrain

A waymarked chalk-stream valley route with field paths, woodland and some slopes. There are stiles, and sections can be muddy or flooded after rain.

Mud and drainage

Important: the Chilterns National Landscape currently warns that high groundwater can flood the section between Latimer and Chenies Bottom. Check its diversion note before setting off and wear waterproof boots after wet weather.

Exposed sections

Water meadows and field crossings are open to wind; woodland portions can be cooler and slower to dry.

Road sections

Mostly paths and lanes, with a small number of road crossings. Do not treat the walk as traffic-free; use care where the signed route meets roads.

Stiles

There are stiles on the full Chess Valley Walk. This is not the right route for a stile-free day.

Food and water

Carry water and a picnic backup. Chenies is the planned pub pause, but there are no shops to depend on through the valley.

Toilets

Use the toilets at Chesham or Rickmansworth station; do not expect public facilities between them.

Mobile signal

Usually usable, but do not make a route decision in a field on the assumption that signal will be perfect.

Pub timing

The Bedford Arms lists lunch from 12–5pm and takes bookings. Check current kitchen hours and reserve if food is central to the day.

Shortening

For the shorter version, leave the valley around Chalfont & Latimer and walk about a mile to the station. Check the live map before deciding: the route is not designed as a frequent-bus walk.

Notes before you go.

Best time

A dry spring, summer or early-autumn day

Date notes

Good for a second or third date when both people genuinely want to walk. It has easy conversation space, but wet boots and a long return are not inherently romantic.

Friends

Best for two to four people who agree on the flood diversion and pub timing before the first train. A large group will slow markedly at gates and stiles.

Solo

A strong solo route if you can read a waymarked trail and make sensible condition calls. Download the official leaflet while still on Wi-Fi.

Food and drink

The Bedford Arms is the only planned sit-down food stop; its listed lunch service is not a substitute for carrying water and a snack.

When the plan changes.

If it rains

If the official page flags flooding, use the higher diversion only with proper footwear and confidence. Otherwise take a shorter local route or postpone — this is not the day to negotiate with waterlogged fields.

If it goes well

Keep the last Rickmansworth section simple and go straight to the station. The long Tube ride is a complete ending; save a second venue for another day.

If you want to leave early

Around Latimer, leave the trail for Chalfont & Latimer station if you need to stop. It is about a mile away, so confirm the walking route and live service before committing.

What not to expect.

Not a sealed, always-dry promenade or a spontaneous pub crawl. This is a real footpath day with live conditions to respect.

Practical warnings

  • Read the current flood/diversion notice on the official Chess Valley Walk page before leaving London.
  • Wear waterproof walking shoes or boots after rain; the flooded Latimer–Chenies Bottom section may require the higher diversion.
  • There are stiles and no dependable food or toilet stops between stations.
  • The Bedford Arms is an intentional detour and may need a booking; carry food so its availability does not decide the day.
  • This route has been desk-checked, not personally field-checked end to end.
