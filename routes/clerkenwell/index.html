Old London compressed into a walk short enough to leave room for dinner.

Begin with one short old-street circuit, take a drink in a verified room, then choose between a proper meal and an easy exit. The route spends money on comfort rather than spectacle.

Best for.

  • A second or third date
  • A friend catch-up after work
  • Visitors who want central London without West End chaos

Not ideal for.

  • A free-ish evening
  • A loud group night
  • Anyone wanting a long walk

The route.

  1. Farringdon → Charterhouse Square

    start · 10 min · 5 min via Cloth Fair to next

    Charterhouse Square, London EC1M

    From the previous point: Leave Farringdon towards Charterhouse Square; it is the most cinematic first old-street leg.

    Cobbles, plane trees and the Charterhouse: enough atmosphere before anyone has to decide what counts as history.

  2. Cloth Fair + St Bartholomew the Great

    museum · 15–25 min · 8 min north to next

    St Bartholomew the Great, West Smithfield, London EC1A 9DS

    From the previous point: Walk via Cloth Fair to St Bartholomew the Great. Check entry arrangements before relying on the church interior.

    An old house, the oldest parish church in London and a large atmospheric return for a short detour.

    Walk from the previous stop (opens in a new tab)Open this point (opens in a new tab)

  3. Clerkenwell Green + St John’s Gate

    walk · 15–25 min · 5–10 min to next

    St John’s Gate, Clerkenwell, London EC1M

    From the previous point: Continue north through Clerkenwell Green to the Tudor gate.

    The Green is not green and never was – a useful Clerkenwell fact to deploy. The gate is the photo stop.

  4. A verified old room

    pub · 45–75 min · 5–10 min, or finish to next

    Clerkenwell, London EC1

    From the previous point: Use a live map for the chosen room: Ye Olde Mitre in Ely Court, The Jerusalem Tavern on Britton Street, or the more weekend-safe Three Kings in Clerkenwell Close.

    Choose only after checking current hours. The first two are historically weekday-led; The Three Kings is the practical weekend fallback.

    Walk from the previous stop (opens in a new tab)Open this point (opens in a new tab)

  5. Optional proper meal

    cafe-or-pub · 60–90 min · Finish to next

    Farringdon Road / Exmouth Market, London EC1

    From the previous point: For the committed version, walk to The Quality Chop House; The Eagle or Exmouth Market are the lower-commitment alternatives.

    Make this optional: confirm booking, price and kitchen hours. If the first drink is the right ending, take the easy Farringdon exit instead.

Notes before you go.

Best time

Weekday evening

Date notes

Strong for an early established date: easy exit first, proper meal if the mood holds.

Friends

Good for two to four; larger groups should book rather than improvise.

Solo

Possible, but the route earns more with conversation.

Food and drink

This is the one prototype where a proper meal can be central; verify price and booking.

When the plan changes.

If it rains

Shorten the street circuit and move between two close indoor stops.

If it goes well

Take the proper meal version or continue towards Barbican.

If you want to leave early

End after the first drink and return to Farringdon.

What not to expect.

Not cheap-cheap and not a sprawling adventure; the value is compression.

Practical warnings

  • Check kitchen hours and whether a table is realistic.
  • Weekday noise changes sharply by venue and time.
  • The prototype has not been field-checked.
